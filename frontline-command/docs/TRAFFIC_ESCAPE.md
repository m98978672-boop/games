# Short friendly-traffic recovery

Infantry already uses an occupancy grid and an incremental obstacle planner. These solve different problems: a planner route can be valid against the static terrain yet point through another person. In a crowded U-shaped scene several soldiers repeatedly press into each other until the normal anti-stuck timeout discards their orders. This change adds a bounded local steering attempt after 0.8 seconds of blocked movement against a **friendly** body. It samples short candidate side/back steps, checks the whole candidate segment against terrain, walls and the current occupancy grid, and prefers nearby free space while retaining some forward preference. The original goal and planner route are not replaced; regular movement collision and speed rules still apply.

It applies only with occupancy, pathing and wall collision enabled, outside buildings and at least 150 px from a doorway, without combat, player control, vehicles, existing group movement or special doorway/yield manoeuvres. A changed goal, enemy contact, changed navigation version or unsafe segment invalidates a candidate. For an unchanged goal and navigation version, attempts stop after 20 seconds without moving 55 px from the first attempt, so a permanent local blockage is not hidden indefinitely by this manoeuvre. Normal failure handling still applies.

Separately, squad AI previously accepted indoor move/hold arrival when its centroid and one soldier were close to the target, even if others were still across the wall. For groups with at least three non-player infantry, it now waits for those soldiers to be within 150 px and have a clear final approach. It will wait at most 20 seconds, stops waiting on contact, and skips this rule when pathing/wall collision is disabled. Thus a permanently blocked or fighting group is not forced to wait forever, but a timeout can still leave stragglers behind. Vehicle and player-controlled members are excluded. A fresh order starts a fresh wait.

There is no neural network or Godot integration here. The temporary steering candidate, attempt budget and squad wait are runtime state. Both save formats were checked for restoring an active order, but the wait is rebuilt on load, so exact deterministic replay and persistence of the elapsed wait are not guaranteed.

## Paired diagnostics and limits

- `tools/crowd-traffic-realmap.mjs`: 108 controlled scenes on six selected open sites from one region of the real map, groups of 6/8/10, both factions and three seeds. Fixed identical goals, delayed soldier thinking, other AI systems disabled; Game.step and live occupancy/wall checks used. Out of 864 people, arrival with the **same existing radius and rules** changed 427 → 574; physically within 50 px and with a clear final approach 583 → 709. Arrival improved in 66 scenes, regressed in **one**, unchanged in 41. For the physical 50 px metric, 61 scenes improved and **four regressed**. Path-failed count 419 → 271; 19 people in the new version had neither arrived nor failed at the cutoff. No sampled wall crossing, pair overlap or excessive step. The cases are correlated, selected and not full games.
- `tools/crowd-traffic-holdout.mjs`: 90 controlled scenes, three wider variants of a synthetic U-shaped wall, two directions, 6/8/10 soldiers and five other seeds, fixed goals and no fighting. 201 → 287 arrivals out of 720, 61 scenes improved, **three regressed**; no sampled wall crossings, overlap or excessive step. Not a representative distribution of map obstacles.
- `tools/crowd-traffic-orders.mjs`: real HQ/city doors and player Command API with live squad/soldier AI, 36 isolated 6/8/10-person move-order scenes, other autonomous systems and fighting off. 234 → 273 of 288 crossed the opening plane; 167 → 195 ended within 90 px of the ordered target on the clear side. Crossings improved in 21 cases and regressed in one; the 90 px metric improved in 17 and regressed in six. No sampled wall crossings, overlaps or excessive speed. The improvement is from squad arrival coordination, not the steering escape, which deliberately leaves doors to existing logic.
- `tools/crowd-assembly-orders.mjs`: 120 additional controlled real HQ/city door scenes with the same player API and soldier/squad AI, both move and hold orders, groups of 3/5/6/8/10, both factions and three different seeds. Other systems and fighting were disabled. 639 → 737 of 768 crossed the opening plane; 477 → 560 were within 90 px of the target on its clear side after the run. Crossings improved in 56 cases and regressed in **one**; the 90 px metric improved in 53 and regressed in **13**. Sampled wall crossings, overlaps and excessive speeds were zero. Selected related scenarios, not independent full battles; crossing a plane is not proof that an order has fully succeeded.
- `tools/crowd-traffic-battles.mjs`: six paired 120-second normal AI-vs-AI runs, all game systems active. Only **one** new steering selection occurred; an assembly wait appeared in ten once-per-second squad samples. One-second samples marked pathFailed 95 → 87, total kills 292 → 285, no war ended. Random streams diverge once behaviour changes, and sampling misses short events. This does not prove reduced congestion, improved balance or win rate in normal battles.
- The final `npm test` passed earlier checks but **failed the final performance check** at 500 units: 22.55 ms/tick vs the 22.22 ms limit. A separate sequential run gave baseline 17.02 ms at 400 units (failed ≥60 FPS) and new 16.10/22.18 ms at 400/500 (passed). Before the squad change, another full run passed, while two sequential comparisons failed for both versions near the 400-unit/general limits. Results are noisy Node simulation timings; no browser FPS or performance equivalence is established. Chromium exercised the normal config, an 8-person fixed-goal real-map scene and an HQ assembly guard.

This remains a partial mitigation. It does not solve all crowds, opposing traffic, combat congestion, all doorway bottlenecks or groups of any size; individual measured regressions and unresolved cases remain. It does not warrant describing the game as fully fixed.

## Godot 4 assessment

Frontline: Command is an existing JavaScript browser game with its own simulation, collision, orders, saves, AI and map, not a Godot project. The repository already contains separate Godot 4 projects (`shooter-2-godot` and `razlom-godot`), but no Godot executable was available in this environment. Merely adding Godot would not replace or automatically improve the live JavaScript game's navigation. A genuine port or parallel prototype would need to recreate or bridge the simulation and its saves, UI and browser deployment, including the shared-engine requirements in `AGENTS.md`, and then compare the same crowd/order scenes and performance. Such a port was **not** attempted or tested here; it requires a separate scope decision.
